## Agenda Items & Discussion

* **Project Pivot & Design Update:** The team held a "semi-emergency" meeting to discuss moving away from the motor-generator idea. They are now planning a fully digital current injection system.

* **System Architecture:**
  * **Power:** The system will run off an AC wall outlet, using an AC-to-DC converter to power the microcontroller and the digital-to-analog converter (DAC).
  * **Microcontroller (STM32):** An STM32 development board (such as the Nucleo 64) will be used to digitally generate accurate waveforms. The board features a hardware clock, ensuring that all signals are loaded and updated simultaneously for maximum accuracy.
  * **Digital-to-Analog Conversion (DAC):** The signals will be routed through a DAC chip, which will require its own soldering board and potentially a separate DC power supply.
  * **Filtering and Amplification:** The analog signals will pass through low-pass filters (to remove high-frequency noise) and current amplifiers to achieve the necessary current levels before being fed into the SEL relay.

* **Technical Uncertainties & Questions for Daniel:**
  * The team is unsure if they can program the SEL relay to use a calculated residual current, or if they need to build a fourth physical channel to supply the neutral/residual current ($I_n$).
  * They need to confirm if the SEL relay's hardware specification requires a 1-amp or 5-amp nominal current.
  * They must determine the specific fault scenarios (like "phase A to ground fault") and reclosing sequences (like automatic sequence lockouts) they need to program.

* **Budget and Sourcing:** The components for the new digital setup appear to be highly cost-effective. The STM32 development board is approximately \$18 to \$26, and the DAC chip is around \$20. The primary costs will likely stem from the current amplifiers and the AC-to-DC power supply setup.
